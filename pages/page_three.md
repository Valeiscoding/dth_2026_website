# Open Source in the Humanities: an Introduction to Inciteful
Let’s now explore an example of an open source software. 

As a current Master Student in Intercultural Studies, interdisciplinarity is a constant in the work I do and sometimes, scrolling into papers and finding connections can be a real headache. Therefore, I have been looking for a tool that can help me with this. And that is how I came across [Inciteful](https://incitefulmed.com/academic/). 

This open source software was created by Weishuhn and offers research tools for academics, like paper discovery and —the star of this website for me— [Literature connector](https://incitefulmed.com/academic/c). The latter allows you to find research and papers that have connected two papers you are looking into. So, let’s say you have a paper on food anthropology and a paper on food semiotics. You can add them to the Literature connector tool and it will give you a list of papers and a graph that shows the connections. You can also save this graph. This is a game changer for all of us that study interdisciplinary studies, as it helps us to find connection paths in several fields.

Inciteful operates under a AGPL-3.0 license, and the last commit was done on November 6th, 2026, meaning this is still an active project even though there are only 4 contributors, being Weishuhn the most active one. Weishuhn also reviews the issues raised, with a total of 7 issues published, all of them have been answered and closed and 6 were fixed. The software is being kept free at the expense of Weishuhn, who is looking into [institutions that want to sponsor the project](https://incitefulmed.com/academic/about) and even considering accepting donations.

This tool has actually been a lifesaver for my thesis research, where I am connecting food semiotics with sociology and culture studies. I find this quite useful and I am currently relying on it. However, if the usage of this tool was not permitted, I could go back to doing research by keyword search in academic search engines, although it would be more time consuming. Another alternative would be contacting researchers on the field that could offer me some guidance and recommend literature.
